Stefano Ratti
Chief Executive Officer & Founder
Stefano Ratti is the Founder and CEO of Chaberton Energy. Stefano brings more than 20 years of experience in development of energy infrastructure projects, management, and professional services. Throughout his career, Stefano has helped companies reach their growth aspirations and led the development of numerous clean energy infrastructure projects. Prior to founding Chaberton, Stefano developed projects for third-party owners, including the origination and development of more than 100 MW of distributed solar energy projects in the mid-Atlantic.
Stefano also built and led a greenfield development team at Enviva, a leader in the production and logistics of solid biofuels, and led, from conception to financing and construction, the development of projects representing nearly one billion dollars of invested capital. Prior to working at Enviva and Chaberton, Stefano held executive positions at AREVA, was a project leader at the Boston Consulting Group, and also worked at General Electric (Power Systems), Navigant Consulting, and the Center for Environmental Energy Engineering at the University of Maryland.
Stefano received Bachelor’s and Master’s degrees, magna cum laude, in Mechanical and Energy Systems Engineering from the Polytechnic University of Milan and has completed executive education programs at Duke University, Babcock University, and Harvard Business School. Stefano previously served on the Board of Directors for the Chesapeake Solar and Storage Association (CHESSA) and is an active member in his community, serving on the Board of Trustees for the Avalon and Brookewood Schools. Stefano lives in Maryland with his wife, Michelle, and their three sons.
Michael J. Doniger
President & Co-Founder
In his role as President and Co-Founder for Chaberton Energy, Mike leads the company’s development, engineering, and business operations teams.
Committed to Lean/Operations Excellence principles, Mike began his career in the U.S. Navy culminating in service as Chief Engineer of a nuclear-powered submarine. In 2008, realizing the growing need for leadership in the clean energy industry, Mike joined a young biomass-to-energy start-up that grew to be the world’s leading supplier of utility-grade wood pellets. Mike held a number of senior engineering, operations and development positions as the company grew to a $1B market capitalization deploying over $500M in growth capital and implementing the processes necessary to operate 7 manufacturing facilities, 4 ports, and nearly 600 people. Subsequently, Mike led a 1,200+ person Amazon distribution center outside Richmond, Virginia, implementing same-day delivery service in 2019.
Mike currently serves on the Board of Directors for the Chesapeake Solar and Storage Association (CHESSA). He holds a B.S. in Systems Engineering with merit from the U.S. Naval Academy, a Master’s in Engineering Management from Old Dominion University and a Master’s in Business Administration from Georgetown University. He lives outside Richmond, Virginia, with his wife, Luan, and two sons, Benjamin and Aden.
Senake Gajamera
Chief Financial Officer
Senake Gajamera is Chaberton Energy’s Chief Financial Officer, leading partnership development and the company’s finance, accounting, and legal functions. He brings 18 years of energy sector finance experience to Chaberton, having closed transactions worth more than $20 billion.
Previously, Senake was ENGIE’s Head of Acquisitions, Investments, and Financial Advisory for Latin America. While there, he played an instrumental role in growing the company’s wind and solar footprint to 1.7 GW while managing an investment analysis and transaction execution team of 14 located across Chile, Mexico, and Peru.
Senake also has held roles with Astris Finance LLC, Jadwa Investment, Competitive Power Ventures, Taylor-DeJongh, and Navigant Consulting.
He holds a Bachelor’s degree in Economics and Political Science from Middlebury College, and a Master’s degree in Energy and International Finance from Johns Hopkins University.
Reason Abajuo
Chief Legal Officer
Reason Abajuo serves as the Chief Legal Officer at Chaberton Energy, where he transformed what began as a one-person legal function into a strategic powerhouse that drives business outcomes across the organization. Under his leadership, the team’s streamlined operations and proactive handling of legal challenges have enabled the company to navigate complex transactions, mitigate risks, and capitalize on opportunities with confidence, thereby directly contributing to Chaberton’s growth trajectory. Abajuo joined Chaberton Energy in October 2021 as Director of Legal & Corporate Affairs before being promoted to Vice President of Legal & Corporate Affairs and, subsequently, to the company’s inaugural Chief Legal Officer position.
Prior to joining Chaberton, Reason served as legal counsel for the power sector at The African Development Bank’s Legal Support Facility, where he managed more than $10 million in technical assistance grants and loans for power projects across multiple African countries. Throughout his career, he has advised over 100MW of renewable energy projects across the U.S. and various countries in sub-Saharan Africa.
Reason holds a Master of Laws from Harvard Law School and a Bachelor of Laws (First Class honors) from Abia State University, Uturu, Nigeria. He is licensed to practice law in both Nigeria and New York State, and holds certifications in financial modeling and valuation analysis, strengthening his ability to provide comprehensive legal and business guidance. He lives in North Bethesda, Maryland, with his wife, Queentela.

Chaberton Energy Projects
At Chaberton Energy, we have 100 projects under site control, representing 500 MWdc (megawatts direct current) of clean energy that we’re working hard to get on the grid.

What’s in a (Project) Name?
Our founder, Stefano Ratti, grew up in Italy, and he used to take in the beauty of Mont Chaberton in the Alps from his home region of Piedmont. In fact, Stefano is an avid mountain climber, and he has summitted Mont Chaberton’s 10,242-foot peak more times than he can remember. This was the inspiration for our company name, and mountains are the inspiration for our project names, each one named after a different peak. The names remind us:
- That just like mountain climbers, Chabertonians can do hard things
- To recall the beauty of nature and pristine environments we are working to preserve
- To aim high!

Project Greyrock
Location: Pittsville, Wicomico County, Maryland
MW of Clean Energy: 3.3 MWdc
Average Subscriber Savings on Energy Costs: $1.6M est. lifetime cost savings
Commercial Operations Began: 2025
Land Usage: 14 acres
Project Greyrock, developed by Chaberton Energy and owned by Pivot Energy, will provide affordable, clean energy to roughly 570 residents and small businesses within the Delmarva service area through a solar energy credit program. The project will help residents reduce their electricity costs and connect to the benefits of solar energy without the need for physical installation of panels on their own property. Diakonia is a Chaberton Cares recipient and local nonprofit serving unhoused individuals throughout the lower Eastern Shore, including Wicomico County. Future Farmers of America partnered with Chaberton to visit Project Greyrock and learn more about the benefits of agrivoltaics. These partnerships highlight our dedication to being a good neighbor while providing the opportunity for residents to have access to clean, affordable energy.

Project Lime Kiln
Location: Fulton, Howard County, Maryland
MW of Clean Energy: 3.2 MWdc
Average Subscriber Savings on Energy Costs: 10% annually

Project Fuji
Location: Franklin and Monroe Townships, Sussex County, New Jersey
MW of Clean Energy: 5 MWdc (1 MWdc behind-the-meter; 4 MWdc community solar)
Commercial Operations Projected to Begin: 2025
Land Usage: None! This project is sited on water
Project Fuji marks an innovative leap as Chaberton’s first floating solar, or flotovoltaic, installation. Located on an active sand mining site, Project Fuji will be deployed in two phases. The first phase is a 1 MWdc behind-the-meter (BTM) system, designed to meet the on-site energy needs of the production operations. The second phase is a 4 MWdc community solar project, allowing nearby residents and businesses to benefit from the energy generated via a solar energy credit program. The flotovoltaic technology, with solar arrays installed on the surface of a man-made lake on the property, makes efficient use of otherwise idle water resources, minimizing land impact while generating sustainable energy. The project is expected to significantly reduce CO2 emissions while maximizing energy production in a dense, industrial setting.

Project Santa Rosa
Location: Potomac, Montgomery County, Maryland
MW of Clean Energy: 2.47 MWdc
Commercial Operations Projected to Begin: 2025
Land Usage: 4 acres
A distinctive feature of this project is the adoption of Erthos, an innovative solar mounting technology. Unlike traditional mounting systems, Erthos is designed to lay flat on the ground. This not only minimizes visual impact on surrounding areas but also optimizes land use, enabling a larger MW capacity per acre and setting a new benchmark for efficiency and community integration in solar installations. PureSky Energy is the project owner.

Project Catherine
Location: Cooksville, Howard County, Maryland
MW of Clean Energy: 4.3 MWdc
Average Subscriber Savings on Energy Costs: 10% annually
Commercial Operations Began: May 2024
Land Usage: 19 acres
Project Catherine combines three distinct solar models: a community solar project, a power purchase agreement with Loyola University Maryland, and on-site electricity offset for St. Mary Coptic Orthodox Church, the owner of the land used for the project. The project involved significant input from the community, St. Mary Coptic Orthodox Church, and project subscribers. For instance, by limiting the footprint of the solar arrays to unused portions of the existing site, project designers retained prime athletic fields for the church. The solar land lease payments will also benefit the St. Mary community.

Project Friendship
Location: West Friendship, Howard County, Maryland
MW of Clean Energy: 6.25 MWdc
Average Subscriber Savings on Energy Costs: 10% annually
Commercial Operations Began: Early 2023
Land Usage: 23 acres
Project Friendship consists of two adjacent projects owned and operated by Greenbacker Renewable Energy Company, with subscriptions managed by Neighborhood Sun. The projects are fully operational as of early 2023 and serve local community residents and small businesses via the Community Solar Maryland program, and Loyola University Maryland via the Aggregate Net Energy Metering program. Like all our projects in the mid-Atlantic, Friendship features a pollinator habitat, which will help restore bee population and increase productivity of surrounding farms. The solar farm also includes an additional 4 acres of native plants and trees planted around its perimeter.

Project Greyrock
Location: Pittsville, Wicomico County, Maryland
MW of Clean Energy: 3.3 MWdc
Average Subscriber Savings on Energy Costs: $1.6M est. lifetime cost savings
Commercial Operations Began: 2025
Land Usage: 14 acres
Project Greyrock, developed by Chaberton Energy and owned by Pivot Energy, will provide affordable, clean energy to roughly 570 residents and small businesses within the Delmarva service area through a solar energy credit program. The project will help residents reduce their electricity costs and connect to the benefits of solar energy without the need for physical installation of panels on their own property. Diakonia is a Chaberton Cares recipient and local nonprofit serving unhoused individuals throughout the lower Eastern Shore, including Wicomico County. Future Farmers of America partnered with Chaberton to visit Project Greyrock and learn more about the benefits of agrivoltaics. These partnerships highlight our dedication to being a good neighbor while providing the opportunity for residents to have access to clean, affordable energy.

Project Lime Kiln
Location: Fulton, Howard County, Maryland
MW of Clean Energy: 3.2 MWdc
Average Subscriber Savings on Energy Costs: 10% annually
Commercial Operations Began: June 2025
Land Usage: 13 acres
The project will produce more than 4.6 million kilowatt hours of electricity annually, which is enough energy to power approximately 400 homes. As of July 1, 2025, 50% of subscribers are located in Howard County. The environmental impact of this clean energy project is equivalent to removing nearly 800 gas-powered cars off the road. Chaberton and Pivot Energy, the project owner, have made contributions to community charitable organizations such as Brightminds, the Community Ecology Institute, and POWER52.

Project Fuji
Location: Franklin and Monroe Townships, Sussex County, New Jersey
MW of Clean Energy: 5 MWdc (1 MWdc behind-the-meter; 4 MWdc community solar)
Commercial Operations Projected to Begin: 2025
Land Usage: None! This project is sited on water
Project Fuji marks an innovative leap as Chaberton’s first floating solar, or flotovoltaic, installation. Located on an active sand mining site, Project Fuji will be deployed in two phases. The first phase is a 1 MWdc behind-the-meter (BTM) system, designed to meet the on-site energy needs of the production operations. The second phase is a 4 MWdc community solar project, allowing nearby residents and businesses to benefit from the energy generated via a solar energy credit program. The flotovoltaic technology, with solar arrays installed on the surface of a man-made lake on the property, makes efficient use of otherwise idle water resources, minimizing land impact while generating sustainable energy. The project is expected to significantly reduce CO2 emissions while maximizing energy production in a dense, industrial setting.

Project Santa Rosa
Location: Potomac, Montgomery County, Maryland
MW of Clean Energy: 2.47 MWdc
Commercial Operations Projected to Begin: 2025
Land Usage: 4 acres
A distinctive feature of this project is the adoption of Erthos, an innovative solar mounting technology. Unlike traditional mounting systems, Erthos is designed to lay flat on the ground. This not only minimizes visual impact on surrounding areas but also optimizes land use, enabling a larger MW capacity per acre and setting a new benchmark for efficiency and community integration in solar installations. PureSky Energy is the project owner.

Project Catherine
Location: Cooksville, Howard County, Maryland
MW of Clean Energy: 4.3 MWdc
Average Subscriber Savings on Energy Costs: 10% annually
Commercial Operations Began: May 2024
Land Usage: 19 acres
Project Catherine combines three distinct solar models: a community solar project, a power purchase agreement with Loyola University Maryland, and on-site electricity offset for St. Mary Coptic Orthodox Church, the owner of the land used for the project. The project involved significant input from the community, St. Mary Coptic Orthodox Church, and project subscribers. For instance, by limiting the footprint of the solar arrays to unused portions of the existing site, project designers retained prime athletic fields for the church. The solar land lease payments will also benefit the St. Mary community.

Project Friendship
Location: West Friendship, Howard County, Maryland
MW of Clean Energy: 6.25 MWdc
Average Subscriber Savings on Energy Costs: 10% annually
Commercial Operations Began: Early 2023
Land Usage: 23 acres
Project Friendship consists of two adjacent projects owned and operated by Greenbacker Renewable Energy Company, with subscriptions managed by Neighborhood Sun. The projects are fully operational as of early 2023 and serve local community residents and small businesses via the Community Solar Maryland program, and Loyola University Maryland via the Aggregate Net Energy Metering program. Like all our projects in the mid-Atlantic, Friendship features a pollinator habitat, which will help restore bee population and increase productivity of surrounding farms. The solar farm also includes an additional 4 acres of native plants and trees planted around its perimeter.

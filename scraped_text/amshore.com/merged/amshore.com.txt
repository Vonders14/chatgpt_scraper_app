Landowners may choose to develop renewable energy projects on their land for a variety of reasons. Using the research we gathered in writing and publishing the Landowner’s Guide to Renewable Energy, we compiled the top 7 reasons landowners go with renewable energy projects:
- Financial incentives: Landowners can earn income from leasing their land for renewable energy projects or from selling the energy generated from the project. This can be a significant source of revenue for farmers and other rural landowners.
- Environmental benefits: Renewable energy projects produce clean and sustainable energy, which can help to reduce greenhouse gas emissions and combat climate change. Landowners who are environmentally conscious may choose to develop renewable energy projects on their land to reduce their carbon footprint.
- Local economic benefits: Renewable energy projects can create jobs and boost the local economy by providing income for local businesses and workers. Landowners may choose to develop renewable energy projects on their land to support the local community.
- Community engagement: Some landowners may want to be part of a community project that makes a positive impact in the area. By supporting a renewable energy project, landowners can be part of a project that benefits the community.
- Diversifying income: For farmers and ranchers, renewable energy projects can be a way to diversify their income and reduce their dependence on traditional agricultural markets. This can help to mitigate the financial risks associated with fluctuating crop and commodity prices.
- Tax benefits: Landowners may also be eligible for certain tax benefits by developing renewable energy projects on their land.
- Long-term investments: Renewable energy projects can be a long-term investment for landowners, providing them with a steady income over time and helping to ensure their land’s economic viability for future generations.

In today’s rapidly evolving energy landscape, renewable energy sources like wind, solar, and hydro power are gaining prominence. For landowners, partnering with a renewable energy developer offers a unique opportunity to contribute to a sustainable future while reaping substantial financial and social benefits. Here’s why you should consider such a partnership:
Stable and Long-term Income
One of the most compelling reasons to partner with a renewable energy developer is the promise of stable, long-term income. Leasing your land for renewable energy projects, such as wind farms or solar arrays, provides a consistent revenue stream through lease payments. These agreements often span 20-30 years, ensuring financial stability for decades.
Diversification of Income
For landowners, especially those involved in agriculture, relying solely on traditional income sources can be risky due to market fluctuations, weather conditions, and other factors. By leasing part of your land for renewable energy development, you diversify your income sources, reducing overall financial risk.
Enhanced Property Value
Hosting a renewable energy project can significantly enhance the value of your property. Renewable energy installations demonstrate that your land is suitable for high-tech, sustainable projects, making it more attractive to potential buyers or future developers.
Environmental Stewardship
By partnering with a renewable energy developer, you contribute to reducing carbon emissions and combating climate change. This partnership aligns you with global sustainability goals and showcases your commitment to environmental stewardship, enhancing your reputation within the community.
Minimal Impact on Existing Land Use
Renewable energy projects are designed to coexist with existing land uses. For instance, solar panels can be installed on less productive land, and wind turbines have a small footprint, allowing agricultural activities to continue around them. This means you can generate additional income without significantly disrupting your current operations.
Community Benefits
Renewable energy projects can bring substantial benefits to local communities. These include job creation during the construction and operational phases, infrastructure improvements, and potential reductions in local energy costs. By hosting a renewable energy project, you help stimulate local economic growth and improve the quality of life in your area.
Government Incentives and Support
Many governments offer incentives and support for renewable energy projects, including tax breaks, grants, and favorable regulations. Partnering with a renewable energy developer allows you to take advantage of these benefits, making the financial prospects even more attractive.
Technological Advancements
Renewable energy is at the forefront of technological innovation. By partnering with a developer, you gain access to cutting-edge technologies and practices. This not only enhances the efficiency and profitability of the project but also positions you as a forward-thinking landowner.
Educational Opportunities
Hosting a renewable energy project can provide educational opportunities for local schools and universities. It can serve as a live demonstration of renewable energy technologies, fostering a greater understanding and appreciation for sustainable practices among future generations.
Personal Fulfillment
Finally, there is a sense of personal fulfillment that comes with contributing to a sustainable future. Knowing that your land is being used to generate clean, renewable energy can be incredibly rewarding and can leave a lasting legacy for your family and community.
Partnering with a renewable energy developer offers a multitude of benefits for landowners. From stable, long-term income and enhanced property value to contributing to environmental sustainability and community development, the advantages are significant. By taking this step, you not only secure your financial future but also play a vital role in the transition to a cleaner, more sustainable energy landscape.

URL: https://www.amshore.com/site-assessment-picking-the-perfect-spot/

Among the key factors to consider in the renewable energy development process is site assessment. A site assessment is necessary to determine if the site has the resources, such as wind, sun, or water, to produce energy. A site assessment also considers the terrain, environmental conditions, and the accessibility of the site. Site assessment helps to identify potential challenges that could impact the project’s performance, such as limited wind speeds or shading on solar panels.
To learn more about the site assessments, we spoke with Amshore Project Developer Andrew Jeffreys, who offered his unique insight into the process.
Q: Andrew, tell us more about renewable energy project site assessment.
Andrew: When we’re looking at sites for renewable energy, we first look at the resources that might be available because the “fuel” – sun or wind – comes from the environment. But the full site assessment includes more than just that as we need to determine if a location has enough wind or solar resources. Then we look at transmission lines that our clients sell their power into. From there, we’ve got several other restrictions that we go through, whether it’s airports, county ordinances, other competing projects, and we want to be good neighbors too. We start working down a funnel: we start with the bigger questions and then we work our way down to important, but more detailed, items like environmental and population concerns. There are quite a few boxes that we check off before we’re ready to say a site assessment is complete.
Q: What differences exist among wind, solar and battery site assessments?
Andrew: The biggest difference is size: the amount of land we need for a successful project. Specifically, we look at the area and the setbacks from other projects, environmental features and structures like airports and populated areas. Size is increased dramatically with wind compared to battery. The differences among wind, solar and battery are really on a spectrum—wind on the big end of the spectrum and battery on the smaller end. With wind, we need the largest land mass but also the largest buffer areas around the project. Whereas battery can be down with just an acre or two. We could have one small enough right in the middle of a city, for example. Solar sites between wind and battery as far as site requirements.
Q: How do developers perform site assessments? What is a negative-focused approach to site assessments?
Andrew: First, we get a clear idea of what we want the project to be in the end. During our ideation stage we think about if the project is going to be wind, solar or battery, or—increasingly—any combination of the three. Knowing the technology gives us an idea of the amount of land and transmission capacity needed. Next, we start to narrow down the possibilities where land and capacity meet. Then we go down the checklist of all the other things that need to be done and start looking for fatal flaws. It really becomes a “negative-focused” approach. We’re looking for the things that would kill the project, things that would keep the project from being able to move forward. Optimistically, we won’t find any, but the reality is they are more common than not. When we don’t find any fatal flaws, we move forward. Next, we’ll do a desktop review combining various technologies, information sources and our experience, and start mapping out our opportunities. We may also need to check out the site in-person. Ultimately, we’ll quickly find out how viable a project is through our site assessment process.
Q: Since Amshore was established over 20 years ago, how has the site assessment process changed?
Andrew: We have access to technology now that wasn’t available 20 years ago and it’s really made us more efficient and productive. Back in the day, developers like Amshore were driving around to find sites, and it was a very tedious process with many hours spent on the road. Now we can get a desktop review done in a relatively short time covering any state in the United States or countries in Europe or elsewhere. Throughout most of the world, we have this this information right here through software which speeds up the site assessment process dramatically. Now, Amshore can look at more areas at a much quicker pace using technology, then go on-site if needed to make confirmation and final analysis.
Q: How do landowners fit into the site assessment process?
Andrew: The role of the landowner can’t be overstated—it’s their land. They are a critical partner, and we must be cognizant that we’d be putting the project on their land, changing the way their land looks and the way they operate on their land. So, they become a key relationship we must nurture throughout the project. We want to avoid fatal flaws with landowners down the road, so a core tenant of our approach is solid landowner relations, open communication and good stewardship of their property. We’ve seen a spike in landowner interest in renewable energy over the last few years, so we created The Landowner’s Guide to Renewable Energy to help educate those who might just be starting to think about renewables.
Q: After site assessment, what’s the next step in the renewable energy development process?
Andrew: After a site assessment, the next step is project planning and strategic selection. Project planning considers the scope, schedule, and budget of the project. A project plan outlines the necessary resources, equipment, and personnel required for the project. Strategic selection considers the type of renewable energy technology that best suits the site, the local regulations, and the available incentives.

URL: https://www.amshore.com/renewable-energy-project-evaluation-key-factors-to-consider/

Renewable energy is rapidly becoming an essential part of the energy mix worldwide. The increased awareness of the negative impacts of greenhouse gas emissions and climate change is driving governments and businesses to invest in clean energy technologies. This is leading to an increase in renewable energy project evaluations.
Renewable energy projects can range from solar and wind energy to biomass and geothermal energy. These projects are often capital-intensive, and their success relies heavily on the project evaluation process. Therefore, it is essential to evaluate these projects to ensure that they are financially viable and meet the technical, environmental, and regulatory requirements. Here are some key factors to consider in renewable energy project evaluations:
Site Assessment
The first step in renewable energy project evaluation is to assess the site’s suitability for the project. A site assessment is necessary to determine if the site has the necessary resources, such as wind, sun, or water, to produce energy. A site assessment also considers the terrain, environmental conditions, and the accessibility of the site. Site assessment helps to identify potential challenges that could impact the project’s performance, such as limited wind speeds or shading on solar panels.
Project Planning and Strategic Selection
After a site assessment, the next step is project planning and strategic selection. Project planning considers the scope, schedule, and budget of the project. A project plan outlines the necessary resources, equipment, and personnel required for the project. Strategic selection considers the type of renewable energy technology that best suits the site, the local regulations, and the available incentives.
Energy Yield Analysis
Energy yield analysis is necessary to estimate the amount of energy that the project can generate. This analysis considers various factors such as wind speed, solar radiation, and the efficiency of the energy conversion technology. Energy yield analysis is critical to determine the project’s financial viability and ensure that it can meet its revenue targets.
Project Due Diligence
Project due diligence is essential to identify and mitigate potential risks associated with the project. This process evaluates the project’s legal, technical, and financial aspects. It helps to ensure that the project is structured to meet legal and regulatory requirements, minimize operational risks, and is financially viable.
Feasibility Studies
Feasibility studies are critical to determine if the project is feasible from a technical, financial, and regulatory perspective. This process evaluates the project’s technical design, environmental impact, regulatory compliance, and financial viability. Feasibility studies help to identify any potential challenges and provide recommendations on how to mitigate them.
Environmental Testing
Environmental testing is necessary to evaluate the project’s potential impact on the environment. This process considers the project’s impact on air, water, and land quality. It also evaluates the project’s impact on wildlife and vegetation. Environmental testing helps to ensure that the project complies with local regulations and does not harm the environment.
Project Analysis and Risk Assessment
Project analysis and risk assessment is necessary to evaluate the project’s technical and financial performance. This process considers the project’s revenue forecast, operating costs, and capital expenditures. It also evaluates the project’s risk exposure, such as technical risks and market risks. Project analysis and risk assessment helps to identify the project’s strengths and weaknesses and provide recommendations for improving the project’s performance.
Revenue Forecasting
Revenue forecasting is necessary to estimate the project’s revenue over its operational life. This process considers various factors such as energy production, energy prices, and incentives. Revenue forecasting helps to determine the project’s financial viability and provides a basis for financial planning.
Bottom Line
Evaluating renewable energy projects is a complex and detailed process that requires a thorough assessment of multiple factors. From site assessment to revenue forecasting, each step is crucial in determining the feasibility and profitability of a project. With the growing demand for sustainable energy sources, it is more important than ever to ensure that projects are evaluated with diligence and accuracy. By following a comprehensive project evaluation process, renewable energy companies can make informed decisions and develop successful projects that not only benefit the environment but also provide reliable and cost-effective energy solutions.

Renewable energy development is the process of designing, constructing, and operating renewable energy projects. Activities include site selection, permitting, financing, and construction of renewable energy facilities, as well as the ongoing operation and maintenance of those facilities.
Renewable energy development includes a variety of different technologies, such as solar, wind, battery, hydroelectric, geothermal, and bioenergy. Each of these technologies has its own unique set of challenges and opportunities, and requires different types of expertise to develop and operate.
The process of renewable energy development begins with identifying potential sites for renewable energy projects. This includes assessing factors such as wind patterns, solar radiation, water flow, and geothermal resources. Once a site has been identified, developers obtain permits and approvals from local, state, and federal authorities.
Developers then secure funding for the project. This can include a combination of private investment, government grants, and tax incentives. After the project is financed, construction can begin.
Once the renewable energy facility is operational, it must be maintained and operated. This includes tasks such as monitoring equipment performance, performing regular maintenance, and making repairs as needed.
Overall, renewable energy development is a complex process that requires a variety of different skills and expertise. It involves a wide range of activities, from site selection and permitting to construction and operation, and requires collaboration between developers, engineers, financiers, regulators and communities.

Powering the Future with Natural Hydrogen
Koloma is revolutionizing clean energy by harnessing naturally occurring hydrogen from deep within the Earth.
Harnessing Nature’s Most Abundant Energy Source
Natural hydrogen is the first new primary energy source the world has found since nuclear power was developed in the 1950s.
Our Latest Updates on Natural Hydrogen Innovation
Stay up to date on our latest breakthroughs and company milestones.
Build the Future of Clean Energy with Us
Koloma is on a mission to unlock the power of natural hydrogen and is made up of a team of innovators dedicated to sustainable energy solutions.
What is Natural Hydrogen?
Natural hydrogen is a revolutionary form of energy, signaling the first new primary energy source the world has identified since nuclear power. It is a clean, low-carbon energy source in the earth’s subsurface made when groundwater reacts with iron-rich rock through a process known as serpentinization.
The Efficient, Low-Carbon Energy Solution that Lies Just Below the Surface
The earth contains vast stores of natural hydrogen – an untapped resource that will reshape global energy. Koloma has developed the technology to identify, access, and produce clean and cost-effective natural hydrogen, which will power the next generation of energy.

- About Koloma
About Koloma
Our Journey
Koloma is a data-driven natural hydrogen exploration company leveraging its cutting-edge technology, proprietary data, and deep industry expertise to identify and produce natural hydrogen in the United States and across the globe.
Koloma is actively engaged in exploration and partnerships that will play a role in global decarbonization efforts. We have the backing of leading clean energy funds and major corporations, and are capitalized to swiftly scale our exploration efforts.
Our Commitments
Koloma is committed to safe, reliable, and sustainable hydrogen—powering communities, protecting the environment, and building a cleaner energy future.
Koloma follows the highest standards and industry best practices for producing and handling hydrogen, ensuring the safety of our employees, our partners, and the communities where we operate. We’re providing targeted training, updating systems, enforcing layered risk management, continuously monitoring safety, and holding vendors to the same high standards.
Koloma is helping build the foundation of a new energy economy, creating high-paying jobs, supporting industries like fertilizer, steel, and sustainable fuel, and delivering long-term economic opportunities for rural communities and energy workers alike. The growth of natural hydrogen will be a game-changer for local economies across the country.
Koloma drives environmental preservation with ingenuity. Our production process requires minimal surface disruption and uses less water and energy than any alternative form of hydrogen production – a groundbreaking change in approach and environmental impact in the energy sector.
A major hurdle to decarbonization is the intermittent nature of most clean energy technologies. Koloma’s method of hydrogen production addresses this challenge by providing clean, cost-competitive hydrogen 24 hours a day without interruption.
Our Team
Scientists. Engineers. Explorers.
We are a team of innovators driven by the belief that natural hydrogen will change the world’s energy trajectory. Our leadership combines decades of expertise in energy, policy, and science to propel Koloma forward.
Executive Team
Pete Johnson
Pete Johnson serves as Chief Executive Officer. Prior to joining Koloma, Mr. Johnson served as Co-Founder, President, and CTO at Monolith Materials, the only large-scale pyrolysis hydrogen plant in the world. Additionally, Mr. Johnson has held investment and development roles across numerous segments of the renewables space, including as Venture Partner at Azimuth Capital Management, as engineering and product development lead for AREVA Solar, and as an advisor to the US Department of Energy. Mr. Johnson holds a Master’s Degree in Mechanical Engineering from Stanford University and a Bachelor’s degree in Physics from the University of Utah where he graduated Summa cum Laude.
Tom Darrah, PhD
Tom Darrah serves as Chief Technical Officer, overseeing natural hydrogen exploration and intellectual property and technology development. Prior to joining Koloma, Dr. Darrah was a Full Professor of Earth Sciences at Ohio State University. Dr. Darrah is an internationally recognized scholar that has made significant contributions to his field through research funded by the National Science Foundation, US Department of Defense, US Department of Energy, the National Institute of Environmental Health Sciences, NASA, and others. He has held several leadership positions within his field, including Vice President and President of the Geologic Society of America Division for Geology and Health, and Executive Director of the Global Water Institute. Dr. Darrah obtained a Ph.D. in Geology from the University of Rochester and has 20 years of experience specializing in gas geochemistry and subsurface geoscience.
Tim Chisholm
Tim Chisholm serves as Chief Exploration Officer. Prior to joining Koloma, Mr. Chisholm was VP Exploration and Production Guyana & Suriname for Hess Corporation where he helped open up the largest frontier oil province of the last decade. He has 35 years of Exploration experience and has led teams opening up 4 frontier plays in 4 continents delivering over 15 billion barrels equivalent of resources. Mr. Chisholm spent 20 years working with super majors Exxon and Shell and the last 13 years with Independents Apache (3) and Hess Corporation (10). Mr. Chisholm has a Bachelor’s Degree in geology from Colorado State University and a Master’s Degree in geophysics from the University of Utah.
Erik Scher, PhD
Erik is Chief Commercial Officer, leading Koloma’s partnership strategy and offtake execution. Erik has held a variety of senior leadership roles in energy, petrochemicals, and cleantech companies over the last two decades. He was an Operating Partner at True North Venture Partners, where he held executive/Board roles in chemicals technology licensors, hydrogen producers, and hydrogen consumers. Erik co-founded Siluria Technologies, where, as COO and President, he drove development and scaling of Siluria’s olefins technology, including multiple strategic investments, commercial partnerships, and numerous commercial plant licenses. He holds a Bachelor’s in Chemistry from Rice University and a PhD in Materials Chemistry from the University of California, Berkeley.
Nevine Nagji
Nevine Nagji serves as the VP of People at Koloma, overseeing the design and execution of human resources for better organizational and interpersonal connection and performance. Prior to Koloma, Ms. Nagji led the HR function at private equity start up RimRock Oil and Gas from inception to sale. She has a track record of delivering sustainable results through people and has supported full life cycle HR functions at public and private companies in the US and internationally. Ms. Nagji acquired her undergraduate degree at the University of Calgary, has studied HR at Cornell and Leicester University and is SHRM-SCP certified.

Natural hydrogen is a clean primary energy where the earth has done the work. Most of it is formed in a reaction between water and iron-rich rock in the earth’s crust through a process called serpentinization. When these deep basement rocks decay, natural hydrogen is formed and accumulates.
Studies suggest that the earth is venting over 20 million tons of natural hydrogen per year, and has been for millions of years. If even a tiny fraction of it is trapped under tight seal rocks in underground formations, we could be sitting on thousands of years of low-carbon energy with Koloma’s technology to produce it already in hand.
Natural hydrogen is generated directly from the Earth rather than being manufactured through carbon-based processes. It can deliver clean, low-carbon energy with only water vapor as a byproduct of combustion.
Natural hydrogen does not use expensive and energy-intensive processes like electrolysis or natural gas reforming. With fewer inputs, less infrastructure, and minimal energy requirements, natural hydrogen can be produced more efficiently and at a fraction of the cost of other clean energy technologies.
Natural hydrogen is abundant beneath the Earth’s surface, with reserves identified on every continent and the potential to supply clean energy for centuries. Its global scale makes it one of the most promising resources for accelerating the energy transition and reducing carbon emissions worldwide.
Natural hydrogen can be produced steadily, 24/7, without the interruptions that challenge many low-carbon energy sources. This continuous availability makes it a reliable foundation for industries and communities.
How is Natural Hydrogen Formed?
Learn about the geologic conditions required to create an environment suitable for serpentinization, and how Koloma can discover and access these natural hydrogen accumulations.
Emplacement
Iron-rich rock forms when magma intrudes into existing rock layers. This “hydrogen source rock” becomes the foundation for hydrogen generation.
Burial
Over millions of years, sediments accumulate and harden into sedimentary rock, burying the hydrogen source rock.
Deformation
Infiltration
Groundwater seeps deep underground along fractures and faults, reaching the hydrogen-rich source rock.
Serpentinization
When water reacts with minerals like olivine and pyroxene, they transform into serpentine – a fibrous mineral. This reaction generates hydrogen gas. As the process continues, more pathways open for water to enter. Hydrogen production increases and some hydrogen begins to escape through growing cracks and pores as gas bubbles.
Hydrogen Trap Forms
Hydrogen bubbles migrate upward through fractures until they are caught beneath dome-shaped rock formations, creating natural underground reservoirs.
Airborne Survey
Aerial surveys over large areas identify regions with hydrogen source rock.
Geophysical Survey
Focused studies over smaller areas reveal dome-shaped structures where hydrogen is trapped

The Koloma Lab
Where Innovation Lives
Koloma’s purpose-built laboratory is equipped with state-of-the-art instrumentation designed to unlock new insights into natural hydrogen systems. Our team leverages advanced analytical techniques to characterize samples with precision, efficiency, and innovation. Our lab uses proprietary technology, custom workflows, and real-time data processing to stay ahead of emerging energy frontiers.
The Only Dedicated Natural Hydrogen Lab in the World
Our expertise and cutting-edge tools give us an analytical advantage to accelerate discovery and shape the future of clean energy.
Our team of researchers and lab technicians analyzes solid, liquid, and gas samples to produce high-quality data. Each sample contributes to a holistic understanding of natural hydrogen systems, helping us create robust models and tools.
We analyze rock samples for their mineralogy, chemistry, and physical properties to evaluate their roles in natural hydrogen systems. This data is used to determine hydrogen generation potential, whether hydrogen was previously generated, and how it may have migrated through and accumulated in the subsurface.
We analyze liquid samples for their chemistry, biological activity, and isotopic ratios. These tests reveal fluid origins and water–rock interactions, helping us understand geochemical processes where hydrogen generation and accumulation are most favorable.
We analyze for major gases (such as hydrogen!), noble gases, and isotopic composition of gas samples, gases dissolved in water, and gases trapped in rock. This provides insight into the history of subsurface hydrogen - such as age, origin, and migration pathways.

Exploration & Operations
How We Find Natural Hydrogen
Natural hydrogen is a clean, cost-effective, and sustainable primary energy source that requires minimal land, water and energy to extract. The challenge is finding the ideal locations to access these natural hydrogen accumulations. Koloma has the world’s top hydrogen scientists and geochemists using advanced science, proprietary technology and decades of exploration data to unlock this revolutionary energy source.
Our exploration process systematically narrows down regions with hydrogen source rock to identify increasingly more precise locations for potential drilling operations. Each step in the process helps our world-class team hone in on the most promising locations with exploration operations specifically tailored for each region’s unique environmental conditions.
Learn more about end uses of natural hydrogen.
Our team of geologists, geochemists, and geophysicists conducts desktop research on a broad geographic region, 5,000 square miles or more. We study existing public datasets, analyze samples from state core repositories, and run data through proprietary algorithms to build out our database.
Our team of geoscientists narrows down results from geologic research and conducts geophysical surveys. We start with aerial surveys covering from 3,500 to 4,500 square miles each.
We use those results to inform our ground-based surveys, covering an average of 900 miles each. Learn more about geophysical surveys.
The geophysical survey results are sent to our geologists for interpretation, and an area of land 5 acres or less is identified for a stratigraphic test well. These wells will not produce hydrogen commercially. They are used for data collection and research.
Samples from stratigraphic test wells are analyzed onsite in real-time in our state-of-the-art field laboratory, and sent to our research lab in Columbus, OH to be further analyzed.
If the results of our laboratory research indicate an accumulation of hydrogen is present, our operations team will return to drill an exploration well at or near the site of the stratigraphic test well to determine if commercial quantities may be present.
If our laboratory results do not indicate the potential for commercial quantities of natural hydrogen, we will restore the land to the condition we found it. Landowners are compensated for the use of their land during operations.

Geophysical surveys use a combination of electromagnetic signals and sound waves to create detailed images of underground rock layers. These surveys help our team identify geologic formations that are most promising for natural hydrogen generation and accumulation with minimal disturbance.

Koloma is actively engaged in exploration and partnerships that will play a role in global decarbonization efforts. We have the backing of leading clean energy funds and major corporations, and are capitalized to swiftly scale our exploration efforts.
Our Commitments
Koloma is committed to safe, reliable, and sustainable hydrogen—powering communities, protecting the environment, and building a cleaner energy future.
Koloma follows the highest standards and industry best practices for producing and handling hydrogen, ensuring the safety of our employees, our partners, and the communities where we operate. We’re providing targeted training, updating systems, enforcing layered risk management, continuously monitoring safety, and holding vendors to the same high standards.
Koloma is helping build the foundation of a new energy economy, creating high-paying jobs, supporting industries like fertilizer, steel, and sustainable fuel, and delivering long-term economic opportunities for rural communities and energy workers alike. The growth of natural hydrogen will be a game-changer for local economies across the country.
Koloma drives environmental preservation with ingenuity. Our production process requires minimal surface disruption and uses less water and energy than any alternative form of hydrogen production – a groundbreaking change in approach and environmental impact in the energy sector.
A major hurdle to decarbonization is the intermittent nature of most clean energy technologies. Koloma’s method of hydrogen production addresses this challenge by providing clean, cost-competitive hydrogen 24 hours a day without interruption.

Natural Hydrogen
Uses for Natural Hydrogen
Natural hydrogen is the first new primary energy source the world has found since nuclear power was developed in the 1950s. Learn more about some of the ways we can use natural hydrogen:
Sustainable Aviation Fuel (SAF): Natural hydrogen provides a clean, cost-effective fuel source for aviation
1 of 6Grid-Friendly AI and Data Centers: Natural hydrogen provides clean, affordable energy for AI and data centers, keeping them off the grid which helps maintain stable, lower energy prices for families and businesses.
2 of 6Domestic Fertilizer Production: Natural hydrogen enables cost-effective, clean ammonia production for fertilizer, supporting local farmers, lowering food costs, and allowing fertilizer to be produced domestically.
3 of 6Reliable 24/7 Grid Support: Natural hydrogen supports low-emission power and grid stability 24/7, working with existing energy sources to deliver reliable and affordable power to homes, schools, and businesses.
4 of 6Hard to Abate Sectors: Natural hydrogen allows for clean, domestically produced solutions for hard to abate sectors like steel, cement, and glass manufacturing, resulting in reduced production costs.
5 of 6Heavy Transport Fuel: Natural hydrogen is a clean and cost-effective source for long-haul trucking and cargo shipping.
6 of 6Clean. Affordable. Reliable. No Compromises.
Natural hydrogen is a clean primary energy where the earth has done the work. Most of it is formed in a reaction between water and iron-rich rock in the earth’s crust through a process called serpentinization. When these deep basement rocks decay, natural hydrogen is formed and accumulates.
Studies suggest that the earth is venting over 20 million tons of natural hydrogen per year, and has been for millions of years. If even a tiny fraction of it is trapped under tight seal rocks in underground formations, we could be sitting on thousands of years of low-carbon energy with Koloma’s technology to produce it already in hand.
Natural hydrogen is generated directly from the Earth rather than being manufactured through carbon-based processes. It can deliver clean, low-carbon energy with only water vapor as a byproduct of combustion.
Natural hydrogen does not use expensive and energy-intensive processes like electrolysis or natural gas reforming. With fewer inputs, less infrastructure, and minimal energy requirements, natural hydrogen can be produced more efficiently and at a fraction of the cost of other clean energy technologies.
Natural hydrogen is abundant beneath the Earth’s surface, with reserves identified on every continent and the potential to supply clean energy for centuries. Its global scale makes it one of the most promising resources for accelerating the energy transition and reducing carbon emissions worldwide.
Natural hydrogen can be produced steadily, 24/7, without the interruptions that challenge many low-carbon energy sources. This continuous availability makes it a reliable foundation for industries and communities.
How is Natural Hydrogen Formed?
Learn about the geologic conditions required to create an environment suitable for serpentinization, and how Koloma can discover and access these natural hydrogen accumulations.
Emplacement
Iron-rich rock forms when magma intrudes into existing rock layers. This “hydrogen source rock” becomes the foundation for hydrogen generation.
Burial
Over millions of years, sediments accumulate and harden into sedimentary rock, burying the hydrogen source rock.
Deformation
Infiltration
Groundwater seeps deep underground along fractures and faults, reaching the hydrogen-rich source rock.
Serpentinization
When water reacts with minerals like olivine and pyroxene, they transform into serpentine – a fibrous mineral. This reaction generates hydrogen gas. As the process continues, more pathways open for water to enter. Hydrogen production increases and some hydrogen begins to escape through growing cracks and pores as gas bubbles.
Hydrogen Trap Forms
Hydrogen bubbles migrate upward through fractures until they are caught beneath dome-shaped rock formations, creating natural underground reservoirs.
Airborne Survey
Aerial surveys over large areas identify regions with hydrogen source rock.
Geophysical Survey
Focused studies over smaller areas reveal dome-shaped structures where hydrogen is trapped
Accessing the Hydrogen
Koloma drills a well to access a hydrogen reservoir safely and efficiently.

Natural hydrogen source rock is very different than rocks that form oil and gas. Actually, there is little overlap in places where it is found. To find large commercial resources, a new wave of technology-driven frontier exploration is needed. Discoveries will be transformational, both for hydrogen markets and for the energy industry globally. Here is how natural hydrogen stacks up against other options:

FAQs
What is Koloma?
Koloma is a pioneering energy technology company leading the exploration and commercialization of natural hydrogen—a clean, cost-effective, and sustainable primary energy source. We’re using advanced science and decades of data to unlock this revolutionary energy solution.
What is natural hydrogen?
Natural hydrogen is hydrogen gas that exists naturally underground. Unlike manufactured hydrogen, it requires minimal land, water, and energy to extract, and has a low carbon footprint. It is a clean and efficient primary resource.
Why does natural hydrogen matter?
Natural hydrogen offers a low-cost, low-carbon solution to support global energy needs. It reduces dependence on foreign energy sources, minimizes environmental impact, and can fuel everything from data centers and aviation to agriculture and construction.
How is Koloma different from other energy companies?

- The world’s top hydrogen scientists and geochemists
- Over 20 years of exploration data and proprietary technology
- A data advantage greater than the U.S. Geological Survey (USGS)
- One of the only active natural hydrogen drilling programs in the U.S.
Where is Koloma based?
We are headquartered in Denver, Colorado, with a research lab at The Ohio State University. We expanded internationally in 2025 by licensing our hydrogen technology in Australia and forming a partnership with the Government of the Philippines.

The Koloma Lab
Where Innovation Lives
Koloma’s purpose-built laboratory is equipped with state-of-the-art instrumentation designed to unlock new insights into natural hydrogen systems. Our team leverages advanced analytical techniques to characterize samples with precision, efficiency, and innovation. Our lab uses proprietary technology, custom workflows, and real-time data processing to stay ahead of emerging energy frontiers.
The Only Dedicated Natural Hydrogen Lab in the World
Our expertise and cutting-edge tools give us an analytical advantage to accelerate discovery and shape the future of clean energy.
Our team of researchers and lab technicians analyzes solid, liquid, and gas samples to produce high-quality data. Each sample contributes to a holistic understanding of natural hydrogen systems, helping us create robust models and tools.
We analyze rock samples for their mineralogy, chemistry, and physical properties to evaluate their roles in natural hydrogen systems. This data is used to determine hydrogen generation potential, whether hydrogen was previously generated, and how it may have migrated through and accumulated in the subsurface.
We analyze liquid samples for their chemistry, biological activity, and isotopic ratios. These tests reveal fluid origins and water–rock interactions, helping us understand geochemical processes where hydrogen generation and accumulation are most favorable.
We analyze for major gases (such as hydrogen!), noble gases, and isotopic composition of gas samples, gases dissolved in water, and gases trapped in rock. This provides insight into the history of subsurface hydrogen - such as age, origin, and migration pathways.
FAQs
What are the environmental benefits of natural hydrogen?
- Natural hydrogen:
- Produces clean energy with minimal emissions
- Requires less land, water, and power to extract
- Has been confirmed through third-party assessments as one of the most environmentally friendly hydrogen sources
- Avoids the intensive inputs and emissions of traditional hydrogen production methods
Is natural hydrogen extraction safe?
- Yes. Safety is central to our operations:
- We apply industry-standard safety systems refined over decades of subsurface exploration
- We provide comprehensive training and risk management
- We monitor hydrogen quality and site emissions continuously
- Vendors must meet our high safety standards
What is the strategic importance of natural hydrogen?
- Natural hydrogen can:
- Help the U.S. achieve energy independence
- Provide a stable, domestic energy supply immune to weather variability
- Strengthen national security by reducing reliance on imports
- Aid in helium and xenon recovery—critical for semiconductor manufacturing, medical imaging, and space travel
What industries can use natural hydrogen?
- Natural hydrogen can power or support:
- Data centers (AI and cloud computing)
- Fertilizer production (especially ammonia-based)
- Sustainable aviation fuel
- Trucking and maritime shipping (via synthetic fuels)
- Clean steel and cement manufacturing
- Helium and xenon extraction for semiconductors, defense, and aerospace
When will Koloma’s discoveries reach the market?
We expect the first major natural hydrogen discovery in the U.S. within the next two years. This will unlock a scalable, clean energy source with the potential to reshape domestic and global energy markets.

Natural Hydrogen
Uses for Natural Hydrogen
Natural hydrogen is the first new primary energy source the world has found since nuclear power was developed in the 1950s. Learn more about some of the ways we can use natural hydrogen:
Sustainable Aviation Fuel (SAF): Natural hydrogen provides a clean, cost-effective fuel source for aviation
1 of 6Grid-Friendly AI and Data Centers: Natural hydrogen provides clean, affordable energy for AI and data centers, keeping them off the grid which helps maintain stable, lower energy prices for families and businesses.
2 of 6Domestic Fertilizer Production: Natural hydrogen enables cost-effective, clean ammonia production for fertilizer, supporting local farmers, lowering food costs, and allowing fertilizer to be produced domestically.
3 of 6Reliable 24/7 Grid Support: Natural hydrogen supports low-emission power and grid stability 24/7, working with existing energy sources to deliver reliable and affordable power to homes, schools, and businesses.
4 of 6Hard to Abate Sectors: Natural hydrogen allows for clean, domestically produced solutions for hard to abate sectors like steel, cement, and glass manufacturing, resulting in reduced production costs.
5 of 6Heavy Transport Fuel: Natural hydrogen is a clean and cost-effective source for long-haul trucking and cargo shipping.
6 of 6Clean. Affordable. Reliable. No Compromises.
Natural hydrogen is a clean primary energy where the earth has done the work. Most of it is formed in a reaction between water and iron-rich rock in the earth’s crust through a process called serpentinization. When these deep basement rocks decay, natural hydrogen is formed and accumulates.
Studies suggest that the earth is venting over 20 million tons of natural hydrogen per year, and has been for millions of years. If even a tiny fraction of it is trapped under tight seal rocks in underground formations, we could be sitting on thousands of years of low-carbon energy with Koloma’s technology to produce it already in hand.
Natural hydrogen is generated directly from the Earth rather than being manufactured through carbon-based processes. It can deliver clean, low-carbon energy with only water vapor as a byproduct of combustion.
Natural hydrogen does not use expensive and energy-intensive processes like electrolysis or natural gas reforming. With fewer inputs, less infrastructure, and minimal energy requirements, natural hydrogen can be produced more efficiently and at a fraction of the cost of other clean energy technologies.
Natural hydrogen is abundant beneath the Earth’s surface, with reserves identified on every continent and the potential to supply clean energy for centuries. Its global scale makes it one of the most promising resources for accelerating the energy transition and reducing carbon emissions worldwide.
Natural hydrogen can be produced steadily, 24/7, without the interruptions that challenge many low-carbon energy sources. This continuous availability makes it a reliable foundation for industries and communities.
How is Natural Hydrogen Formed?
Learn about the geologic conditions required to create an environment suitable for serpentinization, and how Koloma can discover and access these natural hydrogen accumulations.
Emplacement
Iron-rich rock forms when magma intrudes into existing rock layers. This “hydrogen source rock” becomes the foundation for hydrogen generation.
Burial
Over millions of years, sediments accumulate and harden into sedimentary rock, burying the hydrogen source rock.
Deformation
Infiltration
Groundwater seeps deep underground along fractures and faults, reaching the hydrogen-rich source rock.
Serpentinization
When water reacts with minerals like olivine and pyroxene, they transform into serpentine – a fibrous mineral. This reaction generates hydrogen gas. As the process continues, more pathways open for water to enter. Hydrogen production increases and some hydrogen begins to escape through growing cracks and pores as gas bubbles.
Hydrogen Trap Forms
Hydrogen bubbles migrate upward through fractures until they are caught beneath dome-shaped rock formations, creating natural underground reservoirs.
Airborne Survey
Aerial surveys over large areas identify regions with hydrogen source rock.
Geophysical Survey
Focused studies over smaller areas reveal dome-shaped structures where hydrogen is trapped
Accessing the Hydrogen
Koloma drills a well to access a hydrogen reservoir safely and efficiently.

The Koloma Lab
Where Innovation Lives
Koloma’s purpose-built laboratory is equipped with state-of-the-art instrumentation designed to unlock new insights into natural hydrogen systems. Our team leverages advanced analytical techniques to characterize samples with precision, efficiency, and innovation. Our lab uses proprietary technology, custom workflows, and real-time data processing to stay ahead of emerging energy frontiers.
The Only Dedicated Natural Hydrogen Lab in the World
Our expertise and cutting-edge tools give us an analytical advantage to accelerate discovery and shape the future of clean energy.
Our team of researchers and lab technicians analyzes solid, liquid, and gas samples to produce high-quality data. Each sample contributes to a holistic understanding of natural hydrogen systems, helping us create robust models and tools.
We analyze rock samples for their mineralogy, chemistry, and physical properties to evaluate their roles in natural hydrogen systems. This data is used to determine hydrogen generation potential, whether hydrogen was previously generated, and how it may have migrated through and accumulated in the subsurface.
We analyze liquid samples for their chemistry, biological activity, and isotopic ratios. These tests reveal fluid origins and water–rock interactions, helping us understand geochemical processes where hydrogen generation and accumulation are most favorable.
We analyze for major gases (such as hydrogen!), noble gases, and isotopic composition of gas samples, gases dissolved in water, and gases trapped in rock. This provides insight into the history of subsurface hydrogen - such as age, origin, and migration pathways.

"You're Invited to the Blackout Party!"
If you’ve ever had the power go out in the middle of a storm or
Santa Ana wind event, then you know how frustrating it can be to
suddenly have no access to the home’s electronics. Wouldn’t it
be great to have an alternative way to power the appliances,
even when the utility has a blackout? In extended
emergencies, such as earthquakes and wildfires, renewable energy
batteries can be a life-saver.
Solar power is first consumed by appliances, but any surplus power fills the solar-power storage batteries or is sent to the utility for credit. Clean, renewable energy batteries can be used for emergency backup power during a utility blackout and they can be recharged daily by solar power, for greater autonomy.
One of the best things about Makello’s batteries is the integrated battery management system, which is designed to save money on Time-Of-Use energy bills. A battery can be charged daily from solar power, or lower cost utility power, and discharged to offset higher cost utility power. With a whole-house electric vehicle discount utility rate, even greater savings is possible when charging 2 times per day and discharging 2 times per day to offset higher cost utility power.

Next Steps (after the Energy Analysis):
- Discuss solar panel, plug-in vehicle, energy storage, appliance and financing options. Contact Laurence (laurence@greenepc.com) Charlie (charlie@makello.com, charlie@greenepc.com) with any questions.
- Initial pgs 1-4, sign pg 23 of 'California Solar Consumer Protection Guide'. Sign & date contract. Scan, email and then mail hardcopies and $1,000 check payable to Green Energy EPC by Makello contract address pg 2, OR hold for pickup.
- Green Energy EPC by Makello completes the final site survey and generates the necessary documents for registering with the utility. (Usually within 1 - 30 days.)
- Green Energy EPC by Makello creates the permit design drawings, placard and submits the NEM application to the utility. (Usually within 1 - 30 days.)
- Green Energy EPC by Makello submits the design drawings to the permit office. (2 - 4 weeks typically for response, but could be up to 6 - 10 weeks at peak times.)
- Green Energy EPC by Makello schedules the installation to take place once the site construction is ready. (We are currently scheduling within 2 - 4 weeks after permit approval.)
- Green Energy EPC by Makello Installs the solar photovoltaic system, EV charging and/or energy storage. (Within 2 - 3 days typically, weather permitting.)
- Green Energy EPC by Makello schedules for permit inspection. (1 - 2 weeks typical, but could be as fast as 1 - 2 days.)
- Green Energy EPC by Makello requests permission to interconnect with the utility (1 - 3 days after permit inspection.)
- Green Energy EPC by Makello will set up the monitoring. (30 minute appointment.)
- SDGE activates Net Energy Metering contract and utility bill accounting begins. (1 - 2 billing cycles.)
- Green Energy EPC by Makello returns after the NEM billing is activated, after at least 1 - 2 billing cycles, to take pictures and document the installed system, receive feedback, and respond to questions.
- Green Energy EPC by Makello provides the final invoice accounting summary and the Federal ITC (Investment Tax Credit) blank form, after the IRS publishes it, in February of the year after installation is complete, for you to deliver to your CPA.

Alpine
Alpine is located near the Cuyamaca mountains with a very low density population. It is surrounded by a lot of forest, providing beautiful views and opportunity for various outdoor recreation, such as hiking, biking, and rock climbing. Homes may be more affordable compared to other San Diego areas, and the distance from the city provides peace and quiet. It is a largely rural area, and farms and ranches are common. Alpine lies in close proximity to I-8, so it is still easy to commute to further locations. Many Native American tribes have roots to this area, and some of their headquarters and casinos are located here today.
Communities
Most homes are occupied by married couple families with or without children. A majority of households, about 70 %, are owner occupied, but there is a higher than average vacancy rate among homes in this community. The age of the homes are older,with most being built between 1970 and 1990. The zip codes that encompass Alpine are 91901, 91903, and 92019.
People
Alpine residents prefer a more expansive home lifestyle, so they have moved away from the hustle and bustle of the city. The average age of residents is in the mid to late 40s, and there is not a lot of ethnic diversity in this community. Many residents are married without children, as the children have moved out. Alpine community members are well educated and well employed, but many community members are soon approaching retirement. They take pride in their homes and prefer quality over cost. Residents enjoy home improvement opportunities and staying healthy with diet and exercise.
Environment
Alpine has some areas with medium pollution scores and low incomes as calculated by CalEnviroScreen, representing a population vulnerable to pollution related issues. High rates of dust and pollution particles in disadvantaged communities may increase the spread of viruses, worsening the COVID-19 crisis in communities with already low defenses. Additionally, many parts of the area lie in high fire threat districts. These environmental qualities could qualify some residents for rebates and incentives for Solar Power, Plug-In Vehicles (and EV Charging), or Energy Storage installations, lowering the cost of these installations.

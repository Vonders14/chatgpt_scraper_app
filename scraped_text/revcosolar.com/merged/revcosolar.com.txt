Post-Storm Thermal System Safety Check
Following this winter’s storms and high-wind events across Southern California, now is an ideal time to check on your residential pool solar thermal system.
THERMAL only (no PV).
Commercial pools and HOA-managed systems are excluded from promotional pricing.
Offer valid 1/13/26 through 2/28/26.
Why a post-storm inspection matters
Excessive wind can place added stress on roof-mounted PVC supports, stainless steel straps, and panel attachment hardware. Over time, these conditions may also contribute to wear around roof penetrations. While issues are not always visible from the ground, a professional post-storm inspection helps ensure your system remains secure and properly sealed.
Revco Solar is offering a Post-Storm Thermal System Safety Check designed to verify critical mounting points and roof penetrations.
Inspection & Verification
- Visual inspection of roof PVC supports
- Inspection of panel mounting brackets and stainless steel straps
- Identification of loose or stressed attachments
- Visual inspection of roof penetrations associated with the thermal system
Minor Adjustments & Documentation
- Minor tightening of accessible hardware
- Minor resealing if visual inspection indicates sealant deterioration
- Photo documentation
- Review of findings and professional recommendations
If minor strap, mounting hardware, or penetration resealing repairs are needed, they can often be completed during the same visit. Our service trucks are fully stocked, helping avoid return trips or delays when repairs are straightforward and approved.
Additional labor (if approved) is billed in 15-minute increments. Materials are billed at time of installation.
📌 Commercial and HOA customers may call for customized pricing and scheduling. (Standard service rates remain $225/hr in OC and $245/hr in all other areas.)
Schedule Your Post-Storm Safety Check
To schedule your Post-Storm Thermal System Safety Check, please contact our office or book online. Availability is limited.
What is the Post-Storm Thermal System Safety Check?
A limited-scope inspection for residential pool thermal solar systems after winter storms and high winds, focused on verifying mounting hardware, PVC supports, and roof penetrations remain secure and properly sealed.
Why is this service recommended after winter storms?
High winds can stress roof-mounted thermal systems. Straps, mounting hardware, or PVC supports may loosen, and sealant around roof penetrations may show signs of wear. A post-storm inspection helps identify minor issues early—before they develop into larger concerns.
What does the safety check include?
The service includes up to 30 minutes onsite and covers:
- Visual inspection of roof PVC supports
- Inspection of panel mounting brackets and stainless steel straps
- Identification of loose or stressed attachments
- Minor tightening of accessible hardware
- Visual inspection of roof penetrations associated with the thermal system
- Minor resealing of penetrations if sealant deterioration is visually indicated
- Photo documentation
- Review of findings and recommendations
Can repairs be completed during the same visit?
Yes. Minor strap, mounting hardware, or penetration resealing repairs can often be completed during the same visit when approved. Revco service trucks are fully stocked to help avoid return trips or callbacks for straightforward repairs.
What is not included in this service?
This safety check does not include:
- Full system servicing or troubleshooting
- Leak detection or roof repair beyond minor resealing
- Panel removal, reconfiguration, or re-leveling
- Replacement of major components
- Performance diagnostics or controller checks
If more extensive work is identified, a separate service recommendation or estimate will be provided.
How much does the Post-Storm Safety Check cost?
Orange County: $149 • All other service areas: $169 (includes the initial 30-minute inspection).
What if additional time is needed?
If work beyond the initial 30 minutes is requested or required:
- Additional labor is billed in 15-minute increments
- All additional work requires customer approval
- Materials are billed at time of installation
Is this service available for commercial or HOA systems?
No. Promotional pricing is for residential pool thermal solar systems only. Commercial properties and HOA-managed systems are excluded and may call for customized options and pricing.
Is this a replacement for a full system service?
No. This is a limited post-storm safety inspection focused on mounting hardware and roof penetrations. It does not replace preventative maintenance or full system service.
How do I schedule this service?
Schedule by calling our office or booking through our website. Availability is limited.

California Title 24 Requirements
Title 24 is California’s building energy code. Below is a clear overview of solar requirements for new residential construction and updated pool heating rules taking effect January 1, 2026.
Solar PV Requirements for New Homes
How solar requirements are determined
Title 24 solar requirements are calculated as a system size (kW), not a fixed square footage of panels.
- Based on conditioned floor area and climate zone
- Calculated using Title 24 compliance methods
- Requirements vary by home size and location
Important clarifications
- Gas appliances do not create a separate minimum solar rule
- Mixed-fuel homes are already accounted for
- Local enforcement may vary by jurisdiction
When solar may not be required
Exceptions may apply due to roof limitations, shading, structural feasibility, very small calculated system size, or approved community solar participation.
Pool Heating Requirements (Effective January 1, 2026)
Primary heating source requirements
For new pools or first-time heater installations, gas may no longer serve as the sole primary heat source.
- Solar thermal pool heating
- Electric heat pump pool heaters
- Hybrid renewable systems
Gas heaters after 2026
Gas heaters remain allowed as supplemental or backup heat when paired with a compliant primary heating system.
- Solar thermal + gas backup
- Heat pump + gas backup
- Hybrid renewable systems
Frequently Asked Questions
Does adding gas equipment increase solar requirements?
No. Mixed-fuel homes are already accounted for in Title 24 calculations.
Can I still install a gas pool heater after January 1, 2026?
Yes, but not as the sole primary heat source. Gas may be used as backup when paired with a compliant system.
How do I know what applies to my project?
Requirements vary by project scope and location. Contact Revco for guidance.

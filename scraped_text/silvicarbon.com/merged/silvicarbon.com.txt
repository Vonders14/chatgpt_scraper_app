URL: https://silvicarbon.com/

SilviCarbon accelerates the sustainable pathway to net-zero by developing and advising world-scale nature-based carbon removal projects.
We provide carbon removals for companies seeking to meet their net zero climate targets.
SilviCarbon is developing high quality reforestation projects in Laos SCALA and Paraguay Ecocorp, regenerating heavily degraded land and creating local value through carbon removal certificates and sustainable timber, biomass and biochar production.
SilviCarbon advises some of the largest reforestation projects in the world Carbon Asset Advisory, supporting project owners to generate carbon removal certificates, attract carbon financing and maximise value from carbon.
There is vast global potential to convert large areas of heavily degraded land into reforestation and agroforestry projects through an integrated landscape approach that improves local biodiversity, creating long-lasting social and economic benefits.
By harnessing the power of nature we are creating meaningful change for our planet and society.
“Limiting global warming to 1.5C would require global net human-caused emissions of carbon dioxide to fall by about 45% from 2010 levels by 2030, reaching 'net-zero' around 2050”.
Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC), Special Report on Global Warming of 1.5°C
SilviCarbon believes in the importance of scalability to combat climate change. Large scale reforestation projects are currently the most affordable and scalable way of removing and storing carbon from the atmosphere.
Planting 100,000 ha of forest will remove and store 20m tons of CO2 from the atmosphere, which is equal to the emissions from 4 million cars over a year. According to the IPCC, by 2050 5-16Gt of CO2 will need to be removed each year, using a combination of nature-based and technological removals.
Integrated landscape projects also generate thousands of jobs, improve biodiversity, avoid deforestation, and help to develop the bio-based economy through sustainable timber and biomass production.
We apply an integrated landscape approach to holistically transform degraded landscapes into healthy, sustainable and productive natural systems.
SilviCarbon combines carbon markets expertise with ‘boots on the ground’ to deliver large-scale reforestation projects.
The SilviCarbon team brings together a unique combination of carbon markets specialists with highly experienced reforestation project managers.
The SilviCarbon team have initiated and managed hundreds of carbon projects, explored over a hundred reforestation locations and have been involved in and responsible for dozens of reforestation projects using an integrated landscape approach.
The SilviCarbon team is located in South America, Europe, Asia and Australia, combining local understanding with world-class expertise.
We are advancing our shared mission of achieving global net zero through our large scale reforestation projects.
Want to find out more about how you can lower your carbon footprint and explore realistic pathways to meet your net-zero target? We can’t wait to explore the possibilities of how we can rebalance the world together. Drop us an email at info@silvicarbon.com or fill in the form below.

SCALA
SCALA operates an integrated landscape project in Laos, combining plantation forestry, carbon capture, native forest conservation and restoration, farmer-driven agriculture and biochar manufacturing.
This hallmark project is 100% owned by SilviCarbon and embodies our mission of generating nature-based removals at scale with environmental integrity while also supporting local communities in the area.
We achieve this by applying the combined experience and expertise of the team, staying true to our commitments and setting a high standard for all nature-based carbon removal projects.
Slash-and-burn farming is the largest cause of CO2 emissions in Laos. SCALA addresses the issue from the root.
Slash-and-burn farming is the most common agricultural practice in Laos and consists of cyclical burning of all vegetation in an area of land to prepare it for food production. Slash-and-burn farming ranks as the number one cause of CO2 emissions in Laos.
At SCALA, we exclusively plant trees on heavily degraded land resulting from slash-and-burn agriculture, extensive bombardments and treatment with Agent Orange. We do this to remove CO2 from the atmosphere while regenerating the soil quality and avoiding further deforestation.
Agroforestry provides a sustainable alternative farming method to the practice of slash-and-burn.
Agroforestry is an efficient land-use technique which combines forestry and agriculture by planting trees in rows and cultivating crops in between the trees, or using this space for livestock grazing (also known as silvopasture).
With agroforestry, SCALA provides a sustainable alternative to slash-and-burn farming while also supplying high-quality wood at competitive rates, reducing the incentive for illegal logging in the area.
This model provides safe and productive lands for villagers to cultivate, increased food security, protection of natural forests and economic benefits from wood commerce and employment opportunities.
UXO poses a great risk to rural communities. SCALA ensures the removal of UXO across the entire project area.
During the Vietnam War the US dropped 270 million cluster bombs on Laos, making Laos the most heavily bombed country per capita in the world.
The unexploded ordnance (UXO) continues to threaten the safety of Lao civilians, especially in the Southeast part of the country.
Since the end of the war, 20.000 people have been killed or maimed by the remaining bombs and 40% of the victims are children.
Due to the high costs associated with UXO clearance, the Lao government has only been able to remove 1% of all remaining bombs. SCALA hires professional teams to clear UXO from the entire project area, restoring the land to a safe and productive state.
We achieve long-term benefits through extensive communication and collaboration with local stakeholders.
Communication and collaboration with local stakeholders is essential and all of our projects uphold the principles of Free, Prior and Informed Consent (FPIC). This ensures that local stakeholders understand the implications of a project thoroughly before deciding whether to accept it.
SCALA maintains extensive communication throughout the operation of the project, fostering positive relationships with local communities and authorities and ensuring long-term benefits.
The project provides fair and safe employment in a rural area with very limited economic opportunities and widespread poverty.
SCALA also contributes to village funds aimed at improving the infrastructure in and around the villages. The funds are managed by village leaders with guidance from local governments.
Large-scale agroforestry projects like SCALA contribute to the national economy of Laos
SCALA and other large-scale agroforestry projects in Laos make significant contributions to the national economy by offering sustainable, high-quality wood products to international markets.
Laos offers fast growth-rates and large areas of available land, offering high potential for forestry projects like SCALA to contribute to economic development in Laos.

URL: https://silvicarbon.com/ecocorp-paraguay/

ECOCORP is a Paraguayan joint venture, 66.6% owned by SilviCarbon.
SilviCarbon’s partner in EcoCorp is EFISA, a Paraguayan forestry services company focused on sustainable reforestation activities.
EcoCorp operates a nursery in Hernandarias and provides carbon advisory services to regional reforestation and agroforestry projects.
ECOCORP is also seeking opportunities to develop its own reforestation projects on degraded land.
Hernandarias Nursery
Tree nurseries play a key role in supporting large-scale reforestation projects, as they provide the seedlings that are planted to remove and store CO2 from the atmosphere.
The EcoCorp nursery in Hernandarias has an annual capacity of 12m seedlings which will facilitate the rapid expansion of reforestation activities in Paraguay.
The nursery specialises in developing native species as well as commercial species.
Nurseries provide a controlled environment for seedlings in order to achieve optimal growth and development before they are transplanted to the project location.
Another role of nurseries is selective breeding to produce trees with the most desirable genetic make-up. Selective breeding can increase growth rates and survival rates of trees, increasing carbon sequestration per hectare by 2-3 times.
The R&D that is achieved by nurseries is crucial for developing the forestry sector. These natural laboratories will help create resilient forests that efficiently capture and store CO2 for a long time.
Carbon Advisory
With the support of SilviCarbon’s carbon team, ECOCORP provides carbon advisory services to help regional projects to generate, market and monetise carbon removal certificates.
In particular, ECOCORP helps local landowners develop agroforestry and silvopasture models to allow existing agricultural practices to continue alongside reforestation projects, allowing for land restoration and optimising agricultural output.
In-House Project Development
The Baapa Restoration Project aims to reforest and restore degraded agricultural land around the Mbaracayú Forest Natural Reserve in the Canindeyú Department in Paraguay.
The project has carefully selected a diverse mix of 12 native species to initiate restoration and create an ecosystem where biodiversity can flourish. Being located on the edge of Mbaracayú Forest Natural Reserve, the project serves as a buffer against illegal logging. The project contributes to natural restoration and climate change mitigation, targeting the sequestration of CO2 as the driver to rebuild the native ecosystem.
The project works closely with local and indigenous communities, providing socio-economic benefits by offering employment opportunities and taking a participatory approach that allows local stakeholders to be directly involved in the project development. This way the project maximises its impact on local communities, for example by preserving their access to natural resources and cultural heritage.
The Baapa Restoration Project is collaborating closely with respected local forestry organisations such as EFISA and local stakeholders, governmental entities, and private sector organizations. These organisations bring extensive expertise in native forest restoration, ensuring effective and contextually appropriate project activities and provide valuable knowledge on socio-economic issues and local community needs, aiding in fair and inclusive project implementation while minimising risks.

Below are some of the large-scale carbon removal projects that SilviCarbon has advised recently:
Paracel
The Paracel carbon project is a pioneering large-scale afforestation, reforestation and conservation initiative in Paraguay, expected to sequester over 100 MtCO2 over the project lifetime. Spanning over 200,000 hectares, this ambitious project aims to transform degraded land into a vast carbon sink.
Through carbon capture, sustainable timber supply, and conservation of natural ecosystems, the Paracel carbon project contributes to global climate mitigation efforts. Additionally, it provides socio-economic benefits to local communities by offering employment while also setting Paraguay up as leading forestry nation. This project exemplifies scalable nature-based carbon removal solutions.
Burapha Agroforestry
The Burapha Agroforestry carbon project is a large-scale agroforestry initiative in Laos expected to remove up to 15 MtCO2 while establishing a sustainable supply of high-quality timber on previously degraded lands and contributing to conservation efforts. This project integrates reforestation with sustainable agricultural practices across vast areas, planting trees and inviting local farmers to cultivate crops on the newly regenerated land.
Burapha Agroforestry supports local communities by providing employment and increasing food security through the agroforestry model. This project demonstrates the positive impact that nature-based carbon removal projects can have on climate mitigation and sustainable development.
SilviPar
The SilviPar carbon project is an expansive afforestation, reforestation and conservation effort in Paraguay. Dedicated to generating CO2 removals, producing high-quality sustainable timber, and supporting restoration and conservation, SilviPar aims to transform 70,000 hectares of degraded land into a productive landscape.
SilviPar is set to remove 14 MtCO2 from the atmosphere over its duration, playing a substantial role in global climate change mitigation. This project not only aids environmental sustainability but also drives economic growth and development in local communities, showcasing the power of nature-based climate solutions.

SilviCarbon is developing high quality reforestation projects in Laos SCALA and Paraguay Ecocorp, regenerating heavily degraded land and creating local value through carbon removal certificates and sustainable timber, biomass and biochar production.
SilviCarbon advises some of the largest reforestation projects in the world Carbon Asset Advisory, supporting project owners to generate carbon removal certificates, attract carbon financing and maximise value from carbon.
There is vast global potential to convert large areas of heavily degraded land into reforestation and agroforestry projects through an integrated landscape approach that improves local biodiversity, creating long-lasting social and economic benefits.
By harnessing the power of nature we are creating meaningful change for our planet and society.
“Limiting global warming to 1.5C would require global net human-caused emissions of carbon dioxide to fall by about 45% from 2010 levels by 2030, reaching 'net-zero' around 2050”.
Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC), Special Report on Global Warming of 1.5°C
SilviCarbon believes in the importance of scalability to combat climate change. Large scale reforestation projects are currently the most affordable and scalable way of removing and storing carbon from the atmosphere.
Planting 100,000 ha of forest will remove and store 20m tons of CO2 from the atmosphere, which is equal to the emissions from 4 million cars over a year. According to the IPCC, by 2050 5-16Gt of CO2 will need to be removed each year, using a combination of nature-based and technological removals.
Integrated landscape projects also generate thousands of jobs, improve biodiversity, avoid deforestation, and help to develop the bio-based economy through sustainable timber and biomass production.
We apply an integrated landscape approach to holistically transform degraded landscapes into healthy, sustainable and productive natural systems.
SilviCarbon combines carbon markets expertise with ‘boots on the ground’ to deliver large-scale reforestation projects.
The SilviCarbon team brings together a unique combination of carbon markets specialists with highly experienced reforestation project managers.
The SilviCarbon team have initiated and managed hundreds of carbon projects, explored over a hundred reforestation locations and have been involved in and responsible for dozens of reforestation projects using an integrated landscape approach.
The SilviCarbon team is located in South America, Europe, Asia and Australia, combining local understanding with world-class expertise.
We are advancing our shared mission of achieving global net zero through our large scale reforestation projects.
